Spaghetti Carbonara
Italian
10
20
4
Pasta: 200g (7 oz) dry spaghetti.
Cured Pork: 100g (3.5 oz) Guanciale (cured pork cheek) is traditional, but Pancetta or thick-cut bacon are common substitutes.
Eggs: 3 large egg yolks (use room temperature eggs for easier emulsifying).
Cheese: 60g (about 1 cup) finely grated Pecorino Romano, or a 50/50 mix with Parmigiano Reggiano.
Seasoning: Lots of freshly cracked black pepper and a pinch of salt.
Prep the Sauce Base: In a medium bowl, whisk the egg yolks with the grated cheese and plenty of black pepper until it forms a thick, smooth paste.

Cook the Meat: Cut your guanciale or pancetta into small batons. Place them in a large skillet over medium-low heat. Cook until the fat has rendered and the pork is golden and crispy (about 5–8 minutes). 

Turn off the heat but keep the skillet on the stove.

Boil the Pasta: Bring a large pot of lightly salted water to a boil and cook the spaghetti until al dente.

Save the Water: Just before draining, reserve at least 1 cup of the starchy pasta cooking water. This is essential for the sauce's silkiness.

Emulsify the Sauce: Use tongs to move the hot pasta directly from the pot into the skillet with the crispy meat and rendered fat. Toss to coat.

The "Cream" Moment: With the skillet off the heat, slowly pour in the egg and cheese mixture. Stir and toss vigorously. Gradually add small splashes of the reserved pasta water (a tablespoon at a time) until the cheese melts and the eggs create a glossy, creamy sauce that coats every strand.

Finish & Serve: Serve immediately with an extra dusting of cheese and more black pepper on top.
