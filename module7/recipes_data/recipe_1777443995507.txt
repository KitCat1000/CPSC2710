Falafels|||Mediterranean|||15|||15|||4|||Chickpeas: 1 cup dried chickpeas (must be soaked, not canned)

Fresh Herbs: 1 cup each of fresh parsley and cilantro (mostly leaves)

Aromatics: ½ small onion (yellow or red) and 3–5 cloves of fresh garlic

Spices: 1 tsp each of cumin, ground coriander, and salt; ¼ tsp black pepper and a pinch of cayenne 

Leavening: ½ tsp baking soda or 1 tsp baking powder (for fluffiness)

Binding: 1–2 tbsp flour or chickpea flour (only if needed to hold shape)

Frying: Neutral oil with a high smoke point (avocado, canola, or grapeseed)|||Soak the Beans: Place dried chickpeas in a large bowl, cover with at least 3 inches of water, and soak for 12 to 24 hours. Drain and pat them as dry as possible with a towel.

Pulse Mixture: Add chickpeas, herbs, onion, garlic, and spices into a food processor. Pulse in short bursts until the mixture resembles coarse sand or meal. Do not over-process into a smooth paste like hummus.

Chill the Dough: Transfer the mixture to a bowl and stir in the baking soda/powder. Cover and refrigerate for at least 30–60 minutes; this helps the starches bind so the balls don't fall apart during frying.

Shape: Form the mixture into golf ball-sized rounds or small, flattened patties using wet hands or a scoop.

Fry: Heat about 2–3 inches of oil in a deep pot to 350°F (175°C). Fry in batches for 3–5 minutes until deep golden brown.

Drain & Serve: Remove with a slotted spoon and drain on paper towels. Serve immediately in warm pita bread with tahini sauce, hummus, and fresh vegetables.|||recipe_images/ee286ebb-480c-492b-9469-17d5c8c6b36f_Screenshot 2026-04-29 at 1.26.14 AM.png
